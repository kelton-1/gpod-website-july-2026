GPOD Golf · Conversion Plan · 11 Aug 2026

The leak is
before the button.

85% of GPOD's traffic is mobile, and it converts at 44% of desktop's rate. But mobile shoppers who reach the cart convert better than desktop. The entire deficit sits upstream of Add to Cart — which makes it a narrow, attackable target worth $186K–$373K a year.

Cart and checkout are not the problem — they are the healthiest part of the funnel. Effort spent there is misdirected. What fails is everything a phone user sees between landing and tapping Add to Cart: comprehension, product differentiation, proof, and page weight.

Two facts that set the agenda

Paid social is 54.7% of sessions and converts at 0.61% — 7.7× worse than organic search (4.68%). It is almost entirely mobile, and 51.8% of all sessions land on the home page. The home page is not a brand page; it is GPOD's primary cold-traffic mobile landing page and should be judged as one.

Email drove 57 sessions in 90 days. Klaviyo is installed and idle. The business rents 100% of its demand at 0.61% conversion.

What the audit found

Five parallel code audits against the repo and the live store. Four completed.

The redesign would ship broken

Four defects reach customers the moment it publishes — a 404 on the home page's primary CTA, a mobile menu that cannot reach any product collection, an unedited placeholder FAQ on every PDP, and "John Doe" testimonials on all four use-case collections. The decision log records those testimonials as "hidden until filled"; they are not hidden. Blocks carry settings: {}, so Shopify falls back to schema defaults and the section's own guard passes on them.

Eleven revenue features are already built and switched off

A free-shipping progress bar implemented twice with its $50 threshold already set. Trust badges. Complementary products. Recently-viewed. Express checkout in the cart drawer. The highest-ROI day in this plan is a settings pass, not a build.

Six sprints

One week each. A part-time developer should read these as two-week sprints, halving scope and keeping the order.

01

Ship & Instrument

Metric: mobile ATC rate holds at ≥ 3.20%

  • Pre-flight content gate. A grep for John Doe, Use this answer block and Use this testimonial across templates must return nothing before anything publishes.
  • Staged publish. Rollback theme first, then PDP alone for 72h, then home, collections, support. Revert trigger declared in advance.
  • Instrumentation. gpod.js emits zero analytics events — the quiz, compare picker and pairs-with are all unmeasurable. Nothing downstream can be proven until this exists.
  • Fix where ads land. 301 the legacy IA URLs. Redirect a stale July 4th page that absorbed 2,876 sessions in a window ending 11 August.
02

The Mobile PDP — shorten the decision

Metric: mobile ATC 3.20% → 4.00% · ≈ +$92K/yr

  • Cap the hero image height. It's uncapped, so a 4:5 portrait shot renders ~488px tall at 390px wide and pushes Add to Cart below the fold; a square shot doesn't. The catalog mixes both — this single variable decides whether ATC clears the fold.
  • Promote specs and compatibility. They sit at positions 6 and 7, behind ~2,300px of scroll, despite answering the #1 documented complaint. A JSON reorder.
  • Collapse the raw description — 500–700px of untruncated legacy copy duplicating a metafield the specs section already renders.
  • Fix the remote-pairing contradiction. Every PDP tells customers to pair a Bluetooth remote, including on GPOD, whose own metafield says no remote is included.
  • Gate monopod content off accessories. "Extend from the top down" renders on five products that don't telescope.
03

Social Landing & Speed

Metric: social CVR 0.61% → 0.90% · ≈ +$56K/yr

  • Performance is conversion work. The live home page ships 798KB of HTML (387KB of it inline JS), 288 image tags and 19 sections, behind a render-blocking 426KB stylesheet, with an autoplay video as the LCP element.
  • Keep the H1 — "WE MAKE FILMING YOUR SWING EASY." is good. The promise isn't the problem; the page weight in front of it is.
  • Ship the nav simplification. The live site has nine top-level items; the redesign's four-item IA is a real improvement sitting unpublished.
  • Make support findable. The support and compatibility pages have empty page bodies, so search can't index them — and the FAQ's indexed body is stale pre-redesign HTML, so search returns it for terms the visible page never mentions.
04

Content Placement & Alignment

Metric: mid-page scroll depth +20%

  • Fill what customers are actively sent to. The Compatibility Guide — promoted in the nav, the footer and the support hub — is completely empty. The Instructions page has no remote-pairing content despite being the link that promises it.
  • Cut duplication. The home page runs the same use-case grid twice and tells the founder's story twice before anything helps a visitor choose.
  • Move the quiz up. It answers the single most-documented customer problem and currently sits last, at position 12 of 12.
  • Fix content that contradicts its placement — a "pairs well with" block keyed for the GPUCK that points at the Caddy; a contact form appended to the privacy policy.
05

AOV — Bundles & Attach

Metric: AOV $109.24 → $119 · ≈ +$69K/yr

  • Bundles are the proven lever — $141–$185 AOV against $88 for a solo GPOD, on 1,681 orders already.
  • Tell the savings story. Compare-at prices are already exactly the sum of components; no copy anywhere says "buy separately for $X."
  • Make cross-sell transactional. "Add it to your setup" is a link to another product page — it restarts the decision instead of completing it.
  • Build-a-bundle, using the Peak Design pattern already researched. A bundle app sits installed and idle.
06

Capture & Retention

Metric: email-attributed revenue <1% → 5%

  • The quiz captures nothing. A visitor who just told us their use case, device and priority is the highest-intent lead on the site, and the result screen has no email field at all. Every completion is a lead thrown away.
  • Point back-in-stock at the SKUs that run dry — Pauly P at 1 unit, Base 2.0 at 11, G Bundle 3 oversold. Two capture mechanisms are already installed.
  • Post-purchase onboarding — setup, remote pairing, Sportsbox redemption and the stuck-extension fix. Cuts support load, protects reviews, seeds repurchase.
  • Test capture placement carefully. An aggressive mobile popup can suppress ATC. Any interstitial ships as an A/B test against the north-star metric, never default-on.

How we'll know

Four queries, re-pulled at the end of every sprint, logged in the decision table.

North star is mobile add-to-cart rate. Closing 25% of the desktop gap is +419 orders per 90 days — +$186K/year. Closing half is +$373K/year.

Guard rails: hold cart→order at 47.4% and checkout→order at 49.6%. Those are currently healthy. A change that lifts ATC while damaging them is not a win.

Known gap in this audit

The component and design-system pass — token consistency, container alignment across custom sections, button-variant count, tap targets under 44px, breakpoint drift — did not complete. Every other finding here is verified against code or live data. That one is simply missing, and should be run before Sprint 2's CSS work begins rather than assumed clean.
